What it did

    What the model includes
    • Full nonlinear two-link dynamics with 20 × 1 mm and 16 × 1 mm aluminum tube, a 60 g elbow hub, and an estimated 0.002 kg·m² of reflected rotor inertia.
    • Motor torque limit, a 1.5 ms current-loop lag, gearbox friction (0.04 N·m stiction plus viscous drag), and elbow bearing friction.
    • A 1 kHz control loop with one sample of delay, 14-bit encoders on both joints, and velocity estimated by filtered differencing.
    • Three behaviors: an LQR balance loop, an optional linger after it is knocked over (limp, or with the arm held upright), and an energy-pumping climb back up. An arm speed governor at 7 rad/s applies in every mode.
    • Two segments: the same realism, plus a 4-second rise planned offline by trajectory optimization and tracked with time-varying feedback. It starts only once everything hangs still.
    • Tabletop: two 0.125 m links with the middle joint locked, carrying an estimated 10 g of joint hardware at their midpoint; the unlocked two-segment tabletop is not modelled. A gimbal motor with a 14-bit encoder for the arm, whose speed comes from a model-based observer rather than differencing encoder steps; the pendulum angle comes only from camera frames, with 0.3-pixel centroid noise on a 640-pixel image. Predict ahead replays the model from each frame’s capture time to the present, and the balance command is smoothed at 20 Hz so camera noise does not buzz the motor. The real plant differs from the controller’s model by 2–4%. Calibration lets the pendulum hang still and averages the camera’s reading.
    • Not modelled: pole flex, gear backlash, base tilt, air drag, and motor heating; for the tabletop camera, motion blur, dropped frames, lighting changes and motor cogging. The friction and rotor inertia figures are estimates, not measurements.
